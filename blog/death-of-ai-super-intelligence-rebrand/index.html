The Death of 'AI'? Why the White House and Tech Giants Are Rebranding Artificial Intelligence as 'Super Intelligence'

🏛️
Find Best AI Editorial
// AI Policy & Industry Research Desk
14 MIN READ

Artificial intelligence did not die on September 29, 2026. Its name changed inside one part of the United States government. President Donald Trump signed Executive Order 14434, directing executive agencies to call AI “Super Intelligence” or “SI” in new official communications. On the same day, leaders from several major technology companies signed a separate voluntary safety agreement. The two events created a dramatic headline. They did not prove that today's chatbots have become true artificial superintelligence.

⚡ TL;DR
What happened: Executive agencies must replace “Artificial Intelligence” with “Super Intelligence” in new official materials where the law allows.
What did not happen: Congress did not rename AI, current systems did not suddenly become technically superintelligent, and private companies were not ordered to rename products.
What tech leaders signed: a separate voluntary frontier safety pledge.
Why it matters: names shape investment, regulation, trust, and how the public understands risk.

Did the White House Really Rename AI to Super Intelligence?

Yes, but only within a defined government context. The order, titled Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence, tells executive departments and agencies to use “Super Intelligence” and “SI” instead of “Artificial Intelligence” and “AI” in new correspondence, websites, reports, policy documents, and other non statutory materials, to the maximum extent permitted by law.

Signed on September 29 and published in the Federal Register on October 2, the order argues that frontier systems do more than imitate or automate human intelligence and that a new label better communicates their potential. That is a real policy change. It is not a global scientific consensus, a new model launch, or a declaration that machines outperform humans at everything. Washington changed its preferred vocabulary. The technology did not change overnight.

What Executive Order 14434 Changes, and What It Does Not

AreaWhat changesWhat stays the same
Agency communicationsNew materials use SIHistorical documents remain unchanged
Federal definitionA proposed definition will be developedNo settled legal definition exists yet
Existing lawNo automatic changeStatutes still use artificial intelligence
Private productsNo requirementChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and others keep their brands
Safety regulationNo new binding rules in the renameThe industry pledge remains voluntary

The Assistant to the President for Science and Technology must propose a federal definition and identify further action. Until that process is complete, Super Intelligence has a policy function but no settled technical meaning in federal law.

Five facts that stop the headline from becoming hype

  1. The order cannot silently rewrite every statute.
  2. Existing regulations, contracts, grants, and records remain valid.
  3. Private companies are not forced to rebrand.
  4. A label is not a capability benchmark.
  5. The separate corporate pledge has no named statutory penalty.

🔎 Fact check: “The White House renamed AI” is broadly true for executive branch language. “America proved current AI is superintelligent” is false. “Every tech giant renamed its products” is also false.

AI vs AGI vs ASI: The Definitions Matter

The word superintelligence already has a technical meaning. Researchers normally use it for a hypothetical intelligence surpassing the best humans across nearly every important cognitive domain. That is much narrower and more powerful than the broad category called AI.

TermCommon meaningStatus today
AIMachines performing tasks associated with intelligenceWidely deployed
AGIBroad, flexible human level competenceDisputed, not formally established
ASIIntelligence beyond top humans across almost all cognitive workNo verified public system qualifies
SI in EO 14434A federal communications label replacing AIReal as terminology, not a capability certification

This distinction matters for anyone asking what is superintelligence. Under the traditional definition, it is a future threshold. Under the new White House usage, it is an umbrella for technologies already deployed. The same words now describe two different ideas.

What the Tech Giants Actually Agreed To

The executives at the White House did not jointly promise to rename products. Leaders associated with OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic, xAI, and Nvidia signed a separate Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities. It describes internal monitoring teams, external audits, and periodic safety standards meetings. It is self regulation, not a statute, and contains no automatic fine or license suspension.

Some companies already used the term. Meta created Meta Superintelligence Labs and speaks about personal superintelligence. Safe Superintelligence built the word into its name. OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic leaders have discussed superintelligence as a long term goal or risk. That history makes the White House language familiar, but it does not prove an industry wide product rebrand.

Why Rebrand Artificial Intelligence Now?

“Artificial” sounds fake, while “super” sounds powerful

The administration frames modern systems as amplifiers of human ingenuity, not mere imitation. The new name turns a defensive category into an aspirational one tied to scientific progress and national strength.

The United States is competing with China

Policy increasingly treats advanced computing as a race for chips, energy, talent, and standards. Our coverage of Kimi K3 and China's open source AI push shows why that competitive frame extends beyond Washington.

Public anxiety is rising

Job displacement, deepfakes, cyber threats, and loss of control have made AI emotionally loaded. A positive rebrand can redirect attention toward benefits. Critics see the same move as changing the story without changing the risk.

Investment follows narratives

Super Intelligence suggests a larger frontier than chatbots. That supports spending on chips, data centers, research, defense, and software. The label does not create capability, but it can influence budgets and expectations.

Safety Promise or Safety Narrative?

The important question is not what the technology is called. It is whether builders can prove that powerful systems remain controllable. The voluntary commitment matters because leading companies publicly accepted audits and internal controls. It is limited because obligations are self imposed and enforcement is unclear.

The label also creates a tension. Calling today's tools superintelligence may make people trust them more than they should. Normalizing the word may also make a future genuinely superintelligent system sound less exceptional. Safety researchers use superintelligence as a warning label. Government messaging now uses it as an opportunity label. The argument is partly about who gets to define the future.

What This Means for Businesses and Everyday Users

  • Your tools will not suddenly change names. You can still compare ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek as AI tools.
  • Government search language will split. Organizations should monitor both AI and SI terminology.
  • Procurement should stay concrete. Ask about accuracy, data use, audit logs, security controls, human review, and liability.
  • Marketing claims need scrutiny. “Super Intelligence powered” may describe branding rather than measurable ability.
  • Evidence matters more than promises. Look for independent evaluations, incident reports, red team results, and enforceable commitments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did President Trump ban the term AI?

He directed executive agencies not to use it in new official materials where legally permitted. Citizens, companies, Congress, researchers, and media can still say AI.

Is Super Intelligence the same as artificial superintelligence?

Not here. Artificial superintelligence traditionally means a hypothetical system far beyond human cognitive ability. The White House uses Super Intelligence as a broader name for current AI technologies.

Are ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Grok now superintelligent?

No independent technical standard has established that any public model is artificial superintelligence. They remain powerful frontier AI systems with different strengths and limits.

Did OpenAI and Google agree to the new name?

Their leaders participated in a separate safety commitment. That does not mean every product will adopt the White House terminology.

Will AI disappear as a search term?

That is unlikely soon. AI remains embedded in laws, company names, products, academic fields, jobs, and everyday language. Superintelligence will probably grow alongside it rather than replace it.

Why is the term controversial?

Supporters see an optimistic name for rapidly improving technology. Critics say it confuses current AI with a hypothetical threshold and may weaken calls for enforceable safeguards.

What should companies call their technology?

Use the clearest accurate description. AI assistant, language model, automation, and generative AI often communicate more than Super Intelligence.

Final Verdict: AI Is Not Dead, but Its Political Era Has Changed

The death of AI is a headline, not a technical event. Executive Order 14434 creates a real terminology shift across the executive branch. The accompanying industry pledge creates a real public commitment to frontier safety practices. Neither proves that current systems crossed into artificial superintelligence.

The deeper change is narrative. Washington wants advanced computing seen as national strength and scientific opportunity. Technology leaders want room to build while showing they can police the frontier. Safety researchers want definitions and controls that remain meaningful as capabilities accelerate.

💡 Our take: Call it AI or Super Intelligence, but measure the same things: what the system can do, where it fails, who controls it, who is accountable, and what evidence supports the claim. A new name can change politics. It cannot replace proof.
