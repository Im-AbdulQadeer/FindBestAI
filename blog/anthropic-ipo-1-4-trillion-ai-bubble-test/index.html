Anthropic’s IPO Priced at $83, Jumped 17% on Debut, Then Fell 9%: What It Means for the AI Bubble Debate

🏛️

Published Nov 17 · Updated Nov 21, 2026 · 12 min read
↑ Updated with pricing and first two trading days

The Anthropic IPO priced on November 18, 2026 at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range, and the stock (Nasdaq: ANT) began trading on November 19. Reports put the valuation at pricing at about $1.5 trillion fully diluted. It opened near $111.50, closed its first day at $97.17 (up about 17%), then fell about 9% on day two to close at $88.30, just above the IPO price, as AI and chip stocks sold off again. This guide covers the final terms, what happened on days one and two, why the valuation figures differ, and what it means for the AI bubble debate. Everything here is as of the close on November 20, 2026.

⚡ DIRECT ANSWER: What Happened With the Anthropic IPO?

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on November 18, above the $70 to $78 range, valuing it at about $1.5 trillion fully diluted (reported by the Financial Times and others). ANT closed its first day at $97.17, up about 17%, then closed day two at $88.30. Forbes reported about 980 million shares sold, including a 147 million-share underwriter option, for roughly $81.3 billion, plus a separate $26 billion private placement with strategic partners. Market-cap figures after the debut vary by source, from about $1.27 trillion (Forbes) to $1.76 trillion (CNN headline), because outlets count shares differently.

Is it the biggest IPO ever? It depends on how you count. Forbes and Yahoo Finance reported that the roughly $81.3 billion public raise beat SpaceX's $75 billion June IPO. By valuation at pricing, Anthropic ranks second behind SpaceX's roughly $1.77 trillion debut (Fortune, via The Next Web). "One of the biggest tech listings ever" is the safe phrase.

Why the Valuation Numbers Differ

You'll see $1 trillion, $1.27 trillion, $1.4 trillion, $1.5 trillion and $1.76 trillion in headlines. They aren't all wrong. They count different things, and some were estimates made before the price was set.

~$1.3TFully diluted (includes options and other claims)WSJ and AP, as cited by Forbes

The first-day figures don't line up neatly. At $83 the valuation was about $1.49 trillion fully diluted, so a $97.17 close implies a higher fully diluted number than that, which is closer to CNN's $1.76 trillion. Forbes' $1.27 trillion looks like it counts fewer shares. The sources we read don't say which share count each outlet used, so treat the market-cap numbers as rough and check a live quote. The $2 trillion figure that circulated earlier was talk, not the offered price.

The Business Behind the Valuation

The growth numbers are dramatic, and so are the costs. These come from the S-1 as reported by Forbes, Yahoo Finance, Mitrade and CryptoPotato. One outlet printed a growth percentage that looked like a typo, so we're using dollar figures.

MetricReported figure
Q3 2026 revenueAbout $17B (about $1.4B a year earlier)
First nine months 2026 revenue$33.2B
Annualized run-rate (end of Oct)About $81B (from about $71B a month earlier)
Q3 operating loss$567M
Nine-month operating loss$1.3B (vs $5.2B a year earlier)
Compute and infrastructure expense, nine monthsAbout $19B (vs $4.4B a year earlier)
Compute commitmentsAbout $895B, with about $69.8B due in the next 12 months

One analysis, from Mitrade, put the $1.4 trillion pre-pricing valuation at roughly 36 to 40 times trailing sales, or about 17 times the October run rate. Its blunt conclusion: at that multiple "there is no room for a single quarter of deceleration." The $83 price implies a higher multiple. That's one outlet's math, but it captures the main worry. One more caution on losses: the operating loss above is not the same as net loss. Forbes and Fast Company reported larger net-loss figures ($80.7 billion for the first half and $104 billion for nine months, respectively), and the sources we read don't explain the gap, so we haven't tried to reconcile them. The S-1 itself lists risks including a narrow customer base and increasingly restrictive AI regulation.

Who's Buying and Who's Selling

  • Strategic buyers. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC are buying $26 billion of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price. Many of them also supply Anthropic's computing or buy its models. Private placements like this aren't subject to the usual six-month lockup, per Yahoo Finance.
  • Retail investors. CNBC, as cited by Forbes, reported that about 20% of the offering is aimed at retail, versus a typical 5% to 10%. One outlet says access for ordinary buyers will still be limited, so many will buy on the open market after the debut.
  • Founders and early holders. The Wall Street Journal reported that each of seven founders plans to sell about $250 million in the IPO. Existing holders are selling about 23.6 million shares, and an April tender offer let insiders sell about $4.66 billion.
  • Control. According to TradingView, founders keep control through Class F shares carrying 50.1% of voting power on matters submitted to shareholders generally.
  • Index funds. Index funds may be forced to buy the stock, as happened with SpaceX. Yahoo Finance notes SpaceX fell 15% in its first week in the Nasdaq-100. Yahoo's day-two piece also compared the drop to Facebook, which priced at $38 in 2012 and closed day two at $34.03, and says it didn't close back above $38 until August 2013.

Nvidia's role here is interesting. It's both a supplier to Anthropic and a reported buyer in the strategic placement, and its own leadership just changed. See our coverage of Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia.

Why Wall Street Calls It a Bubble Test

The IPO landed in the middle of an AI selloff. The Nasdaq closed in correction territory on November 16, per TradingView. Nvidia reported earnings on November 17 and, per TechTimes, closed down about 2.9% the next day. On November 20, Yahoo Finance reported Nvidia fell 12% after Reuters reported an SEC probe of its accounting (a reported probe, not a finding), Broadcom fell 12% after a report that Google cut plans for Broadcom AI chips, and the Nasdaq 100 was down about 4% for much of the afternoon. Anthropic's pop on day one, then its drop on day two, gave both camps something to point at. Read our report on the earlier AI stock selloff after the midterms for how the week before began.

✓ THE BULL CASE
✓Demand was strong. The deal priced above range and the stock closed day one about 17% above $83.
✓Revenue is exploding. Run-rate went from about $71B to $81B in a month.
✓Operating losses are shrinking. The nine-month operating loss fell from $5.2B to $1.3B (net-loss figures reported by Forbes and Fast Company are far larger).
✓A rare benchmark. Dan Ives called it the first time a US frontier lab carries a standalone public market price.
✗ THE BEAR CASE
✗A rich price. Even before the pop, about 36 to 40x trailing sales left little room for a slowdown.
✗Huge commitments. About $895B in compute obligations versus about $17B of quarterly revenue.
✗A "strategic" price. Buyers like Nvidia and Google also sell to Anthropic, so the price may not be a pure market signal.
✗Weak IPO history. Per Jay Ritter's data, about 60% of 9,000+ IPOs from 1975 to 2021 were flat or lower three years after the first-day close. Day two fell 9%, though it still closed above the IPO price.

What Analysts Are Saying

  • Dan Ives (Yorkville Ives & Co.) framed it as the first time a US frontier lab carries a standalone public market price.
  • Mitrade's analysis argued the public market has spent six weeks marking AI down while private capital marks Anthropic up, and that the clearing price "is a strategic price." It advised watching the first-day close, not the first print.
  • CryptoPotato's preview listed the risks as a valuation with little room for disappointment, heavy infrastructure spending, and competition from OpenAI and Google. It also noted strong demand doesn't guarantee a strong debut.
  • Dan Ives, per Stocktwits, pointed to a "perfect storm" behind the day-two drop in tech. A Yahoo Finance headline quoted a strategist warning against "chasing the IPO." We saw those two headlines and snippets, not full pieces.

These are paraphrases from the linked pieces. Check the originals before quoting.

What This Does NOT Mean

  • It doesn't mean the stock will keep rising. Day one rose about 17%, then day two gave back about 9%.
  • It doesn't mean there is one valuation. $1.27 trillion, $1.5 trillion and $1.76 trillion all appear, depending on the date and share count.
  • It doesn't mean a bubble is proven, or ruled out. The IPO is a data point, not a verdict.
  • It doesn't mean you can easily buy at the IPO price. Most allocations go to institutions and existing backers.
  • It isn't a prediction. Nobody, including us, knows where ANT trades next week. The stock closed day two only about 6% above its IPO price.

What It Means for Claude Users

Nothing in the reporting suggests Claude is changing for users because of the IPO. A public listing mostly changes who owns the company and how much money it has, and it brings quarterly reporting. Our own read, flagged as analysis: more capital could mean more computing capacity, while public-market pressure could make pricing and profitability a bigger focus. Neither is confirmed.

What to Watch Next

  • Whether $83 holds. The stock touched $83.05 on day two before bouncing. A close below the IPO price would feed bubble talk.
  • The AI chip sell-off. Nvidia and Broadcom both fell about 12% on November 20 on reports. Whether those reports hold up will move the whole sector.
  • The first week. Index inclusion and fund buying could move the stock, as with SpaceX.
  • The lockup and placement terms. The $26 billion private placement isn't subject to the usual six-month lockup, per Yahoo Finance.
  • First earnings as a public company. Can growth keep outrunning compute costs? Anthropic is also reported to have about $6.2 billion of debt (AP, via The Next Web) and over $200 billion in cash after the offering (FT).
  • The rest of the AI IPO pipeline. A strong debut could reopen delayed listings. A volatile one may make issuers wait.

Frequently Asked Questions

When did the Anthropic IPO happen?

The Anthropic IPO priced on Wednesday, November 18, 2026 and the stock started trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday, November 19 under the ticker ANT.

What was Anthropic's IPO price?

Anthropic priced at $83 a share, above the marketed $70 to $78 range. Bloomberg reported it first, citing people familiar, and other outlets confirmed. Forbes reported about 980 million shares sold including a 147 million-share underwriter option, for about $81.3 billion. The Next Web cited at least $68 billion before extra shares.

What is Anthropic's IPO valuation?

About $1.49 trillion fully diluted at the $83 price, per the Financial Times via The Next Web, and about $1.5 trillion in other reports. After the first day, Forbes put the market cap near $1.27 trillion at the $97.17 close while a CNN headline said $1.76 trillion. The outlets appear to count shares differently, and the filing states no valuation. The previous private valuation was $965 billion.

What is Anthropic's stock ticker?

Anthropic's Class A shares trade on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. Before the listing, private-market quotes used the placeholder ANTH.PVT.

Is the Anthropic IPO the biggest IPO ever?

It depends on how you count. Forbes and Yahoo Finance said the roughly $81.3 billion public raise beat SpaceX's $75 billion IPO, but SpaceX reached $86.2 billion once extra shares were sold. By valuation at pricing, Anthropic ranks second behind SpaceX, per Fortune via The Next Web. It's best described as one of the largest tech listings ever.

Can regular investors buy Anthropic stock?

Yes, on the Nasdaq under ANT like any listed stock, through most brokers. IPO allocations at $83 were limited: CNBC, as cited by Forbes, said about 20% of the offering was aimed at retail, and JPMorgan led US retail allocation. Anyone buying now pays the market price, not $83.

How did Anthropic stock do on its first two days?

Day one (Nov 19): opened near $111.50, reached about $115.10 and closed at $97.17, up about 17% from the $83 IPO price (Forbes, Yahoo Finance). Day two (Nov 20): fell almost 10% before the bell, touched $83.05, then closed at $88.30, about 9% below Thursday's close and about 6% above the IPO price (Yahoo Finance). AI and chip stocks sold off broadly that day.

Is Anthropic profitable?

Not on an operating basis. Reports say Anthropic lost $567 million from operations in Q3 and $1.3 billion in the first nine months of 2026, down from $5.2 billion a year earlier. Net-loss figures reported elsewhere are much larger ($80.7 billion for the first half per Forbes, $104 billion for nine months per Fast Company), and we couldn't find an explanation of the gap. Annualized revenue run rate is about $81 billion.

Who controls Anthropic after the IPO?

According to TradingView, founders keep control through Class F shares with 50.1% of voting power on matters put to shareholders generally. The $26 billion strategic placement is non-voting Class N stock.

Should I buy Anthropic stock?

We can't tell you that. We're not financial advisors, and this is news, not advice. The stock fell about 9% on day two and analysts disagree on valuation and risk. Read the prospectus and consider a licensed advisor for your own situation.

Our Verdict

// OUR TAKE — THE ANTHROPIC IPO

The first two days gave both sides something. Demand was strong enough to price above range and pop 17%, which says investors still want AI exposure. Day two, closing 9% lower and briefly within five cents of the IPO price, says that appetite is nervous when chip stocks fall. The growth is real by the numbers reported, but the valuation leaves little room for a stumble, the compute commitments are enormous, and the net-loss figures need explaining. Two trading days don't settle the bubble debate. Watch whether $83 holds and how the first earnings look. Whatever happens, it will shape how Wall Street values every AI company that follows.
